INVARIANT-BASED LIVE-CORPUS AUDIT. Assert invariants over whatever checkpoint corpus is present, with NO CARDINALITY PINNED ANYWHERE.

A record count changes on every checkpoint-writing session, so a pinned literal fails for reasons unrelated to the contract it guards - a brittleness defect, not a coverage gain.

INVARIANTS: (1) every record under .backlogit/checkpoints/ classifies into exactly one bucket - valid-with-hint, CHECKPOINT_LEGACY_HINTLESS_RESOLVED, or fail-closed candidate - and no record is unclassified; (2) the FAIL-CLOSED BUCKET IS EMPTY, which is the property that actually matters, because a non-empty bucket means startup halts; (3) every CHECKPOINT_LEGACY_HINTLESS_RESOLVED record has status: resolved, and an active record is never in that bucket at any corpus size - it lands in the fail-closed bucket under CHECKPOINT_ACTIVE_HINTLESS; (4) the classification is TOTAL AND DETERMINISTIC, so running it twice over the same corpus yields identical results; (5) the legacy set is MONOTONICALLY NON-INCREASING WITH RESPECT TO NEWLY AUTHORED RECORDS - no record authored after 172.009-T enables enforcement may enter it, expressing "the exemption cannot silently grow" as an invariant over AUTHORSHIP rather than over CARDINALITY; (6) the reported legacy set is enumerated by filename and count in the test output so growth is visible in CI logs, with the count OBSERVED AND REPORTED, NEVER ASSERTED AGAINST A LITERAL.

SYNTHETIC FIXTURE CORPORA cover the shapes the live corpus may not contain - an active hintless record, a whitespace-only hint, a legacy payload with no schema_version - so coverage does not depend on what the repository happens to hold on a given day.
